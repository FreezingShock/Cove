Three ways to open an app, one search.

The full-screen grid, the quick panel from the Dock, and search all share one field and one icon cache. This page covers what each part does, what it leaves out, and where its settings live.

  • Full-screen grid
  • Search
  • Edit mode and folders
  • Quick panel
  • Shortcuts
  • Settings
  • Accessibility

Every installed app, in one grid.

Inlet reads the apps in /Applications, ~/Applications and /System/Applications, including the folders inside them. Each app appears with its own macOS icon. Click an icon to open the app. Escape closes the grid, and so does a click outside it.

  • 5 to 9 columns, 7 by default
  • Swipe between pages of apps
  • Groups filter the grid. Start from six presets, or make your own
  • New apps appear when you switch back to Inlet

Arrange the grid your way.

The grid starts in alphabetical order. Changes you make are saved on your Mac and come back after a relaunch.

Edit mode

Press and hold an icon to enter Edit mode, then drag icons to put them in any order. Choose Done when you finish.

Folders

Group related apps into a folder. A folder holds up to nine apps, and folders can't sit inside other folders.

Pages

Apps run across pages. Swipe between them, and each page keeps the layout you set.

Hidden apps

In Edit mode, tap the minus badge to hide a third-party app from the grid. Apple's built-in apps can't be hidden. An Undo button appears straight away, and a Hidden apps list lets you restore it later to its old place.

One field for apps and files.

Start typing and the grid filters as you go. The best match is highlighted, and matching files from Spotlight appear beneath the apps. Inlet keeps no search index of its own, so anything Spotlight can find, Inlet can find.

  • Return opens the top match
  • Command Return shows a file in Finder
  • Arrow keys move through the results

A small panel that rises from the Dock.

Click the Inlet icon in the Dock, or press Control Option Space, and a compact panel opens. It shows your recent apps, your recent files, and a Show All Apps button that opens the full grid. Recent apps are the ones you have opened through Inlet.

  • Recent apps: 4, 8 or 12
  • Recent files: 0 to 5
  • Dock click opens the quick panel or the full grid, your choice

Two shortcuts that work from any app.

Inlet registers two global shortcuts. Both can be changed in Settings, Keyboard. Press the keys you want, and press Escape to cancel. If another app already uses a shortcut, Inlet refuses it.

⌥Space

Show all apps

Opens the full-screen grid. This is the default.

⌃⌥Space

Quick panel

Opens the quick panel above the Dock. This is the default.

⌃andSpace

Shortcuts Inlet avoids

Control Space switches input sources and Command Space opens Spotlight, so neither is a default.

A trade-off to know about. Option Space normally types a non-breaking space in text fields. While Inlet runs, its grid shortcut takes that combination everywhere. If that gets in your way, change the shortcut in Settings, Keyboard.

Settings in eight panes.

Open Settings from the app menu or with Command Comma. The panes use standard macOS controls, so they behave the way the rest of your Mac does.

  • GeneralOpen at login, menu bar icon, close after launch, Escape behavior
  • KeyboardThe grid and quick panel shortcuts
  • AppearanceLight, Dark, or Auto, which follows your Mac
  • GridNumber of columns, from 5 to 9
  • SearchWhether files appear, and how many, from 0 to 8
  • Quick PanelDock click action, recent apps and recent files
  • PrivacyErase launch history
  • AboutVersion, apps found, last scan, and update checks

Follows your Mac's display settings.

Inlet reads the macOS accessibility settings and adjusts to them. Nothing needs to be set up inside Inlet.

Reduce Motion

Animations become a short fade. Nothing scales or bounces.

Reduce Transparency

The glass panels switch to a solid fill with no blur.

Increase Contrast

Secondary text becomes full strength, and dividers are easier to see.

Almost everything runs on your Mac.

The only network request is an automatic update check, and you can turn it off. Read the full privacy policy for the details.
