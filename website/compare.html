Inlet, Spotlight and Launchpad.What each one is for.

Launchpad is gone from macOS 26 Tahoe. Spotlight is still there, with an Apps view of its own. Inlet is a full-screen grid of your apps, with one search field for apps and files on top. It uses Spotlight's index for files.

Spotlight: fast when you know the name.

If you know the name of an app or a file, Spotlight is quick to type and needs nothing extra. Inlet's file results come from Spotlight, so anything Spotlight finds, Inlet can find too. Inlet keeps no index of its own.

  • Spotlight is built into macOS
  • Inlet shows the whole set of apps at once
  • Inlet is built to be looked at, not just searched

When Spotlight is enough. If you only open one or two apps and always know their names, Spotlight does the job. Inlet is for when you want to see your apps, arrange them, or open one from the Dock without searching first.

Launchpad: gone in macOS 26.

Launchpad gave you a full-screen grid of your apps, with folders and pages. Apple removed it in macOS 26 Tahoe, and its Apps view now sits inside Spotlight. Inlet brings back the full-screen grid with folders and pages, and it adds a search field for files.

Inlet is for macOS 26 and later, the version that dropped Launchpad.

What Inlet adds.

One field for apps and files

Type once. Apps and matching files appear together, and Return opens the top match.

A grid you arrange

Reorder apps, group them into folders of up to nine, and swipe between pages. The layout is saved on your Mac.

A panel at the Dock

Click the Inlet icon for recent apps and recent files, without opening the full grid.

A quieter grid

Hide third-party apps you never open. Apple's own apps can't be hidden, and the Hidden list restores anything you take out.

No second index

File search uses the Spotlight index that macOS already keeps. Inlet doesn't copy your files anywhere.
